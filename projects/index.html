Personal Comic Project

Kimia's Hands-On Guide to Magic

This comic is a passion project I've been doing on-and-off work on since mid-2025. The comic is a coming-of-age fantasy adventure story that follows a young rogue witch as she tries to find family, purpose, and a place she belongs. The story is meant to be pretty casual and personal, and is named after the protagonist's handsy powers. Haha, get it?

Below, you'll find my process notes, some drafts and ideations, and whatever else I've decided to throw in here. As I finish more chapters and pages, I'll throw them on this site, but it's kind of a pain to update so don't expect super regular upkeep. Peace.

Hello

Timeline/Where it started

I began creating ideas for a comic about a small elf witch in mid-2024. During that time, I still had no idea what exactly I was doing. The plan at the time was just a big series of drawings following this little witch and her mishaps, but it evolved into something bigger. Below, you'll see how this evolution happened.

01 - Mid 2024

Kimia sheet

The first character sheet I made for Kimia, the protagonist. Actually, probably one of the first character sheets I've ever made. I tried developing an interesting character, but probably fell into a lot of the common pitfalls of self-insert like characters back then. I also hadn't really figured out my style yet, so I struggled to draw her consistently.

02 - Mid 2024

Polished Sheet

The only reason I actually created this sheet was because I wanted a proper look for Kimia during Art fight. Regardless, I took this opportunity to experiment with and develop my rendering style, as well as figure out exactly what kind of magic Kimia wields. She was definitely more of a kawaii, young adult-ish character back then. Funny in hindsight, honestly.

03 - Mid 2024

Aspen Sheet

I added Aspen because I wanted to draw a cat girl. Yeah. That's literally it. Her character wasn't very thought through and the only feature from her past was that she worked in a stall or something. A very far cry from her grittier past and personality nowadays. Regardless, with Aspen's introduction, the crew began coming together.

04 - Mid 2024

Caspian "Sheet"

At this point, I had some inklings of an idea about a team of ragtag misfits travelling the country and going on adventures together. When I drew Caspian, I had figured that he and Aspen were a criminal duo before meeting with Kimia. Caspian's character is probably the most unchanged, though his appearance underwent major development to distance him from the Rito. Please don't sure me.

05 - Mid 2024

Volgoth Sheet

Visually, Volgoth is the most unchanged character, and also probably the best design I came up with in this batch of artworks. Volgoth was also my most interesting character. His past was a mystery. His abilities were unexplained, but that was by intention. I'm still not sure what role he was meant to serve in the story back then, other than being the tall, strong and gentle figure of the party.

06 - Late 2024

Tree

This was among the earliest environment works I made exploring Kimia's adventure and the world she inhabits. This was my favorite piece for a very long time, and exhibits my proclivity towards creating warm, sunset-lit pieces. I eventually remastered this for a comic page in the future.

07 - Late 2024

View

Another environment exploration piece. I think this one's self explanatory, but still, I was very happy with how it turned out. Also remastered in the future.

08 - Late 2024

Sky

Third of my environmental Kimia pieces. This one took ages to produce, and the result was a piece I liked enough to use as my iPad's background for about a year. While I never directly remastered this one, I certainly drew inspiration for the comet page in Chapter 3.

09 - Late 2024

Fist

This was probably the first piece where I establish Kimia's abilities and try to figure out how her handy magic would function visually. The idea was already floating around; by manipulating mana with great precision, Kimia could imitate the magical "circuits" running through her body to create tangible fascimiles of her limbs. Here, she's probably using it to squash a bug or something.

10 - Mid 2025

Design Update and Big Pivot

After about half a year of waiting around and doing nothing, in 2025, I finally decided to screw it and just get to drawing the comic. By this point, my art style had changed to the point where I could no longer take Kimia's old design seriously, so I commenced a complete redesign of every character to better fit my updated vision. For Kimia, given her already existing scheme, red felt like a better color to use. Since she lived in the forests, school uniform-like attire also no longer felt appropriate, so I gave her ragtag, too-small clothes instead. Finally, I made her smaller, more ragged, and more "scrunkly" to fit the feeling of a tiny witch in a big, strange world.

11 - Mid 2025

Caspian and Gale Redesign

Since Kimia was now adapted to a grittier, less kawaii tone, the rest of the main cast naturally followed. Gale (Aspen's pseudonym) was made more muscular to fit her role as a criminal, given tattoos, a more cat-like build, and a darker past. Caspian, though largely unchanged in terms of story, was now outfitted with trinkets, was associated with a distinct species of bird, and had a much stronger visual identity (beyond just being some bird adventurer).

12 - Mid 2025

Enemy Concepts

Following the crew's redesign, it felt appropriate to also go ahead and make some enemies for them. Realistically, these were probably drawn before the crew's redesign, but it wouldn't make much sense for the site. Anyways, these characters are just floating around now and won't actually appear in the story until another 50 chapters or so. Knowing my pace, it'll be years before we get there, at which point these guys will get another redesign.

About BEET

BEET is a hackathon project I worked on alongside some other friends. Among our team is a music guy and two code guys. I assumed the role of the asset and concept artist, so most of the assets you see in game were created by me. Due to time restraints, I didn't do any UI work, but we'll get around to that.

The game itself is the manifestation of a dream game idea my friend conjured up one day. We decided there was no better time than a hackathon to finally assemble a team and begin work on it. In this game, you play as a young traveller exploring a broken, sad world. The wildlife has been corrupted and music is gone.

There are two primary gameplay loops; a combat and platformer oriented one that takes place in a 2D sidescrolling environment, and an isometric town builder kind of like My Singing Monsters but with plants. For legal reasons, these are entirely distinct concepts.

For fun mini game

Poker Game

Poker Game is a tiny python game I created as an offshoot of a class assignment. Our final task was to create a card game using python, and our group chose Texas Hold'em Poker. I wasn't satisfied with what we presented, so in my own time, I created this.

You can think of Poker Game as a visual experiment. I wanted to see what I could accomplish using simple transforms and static sprites. The end result is something I'm actually pretty happy with. In terms of code, this project is really rough around the edges (I'm not the most competent coder, so most of this was vibe coded), but at the very least it serves as a neat visual reference for a future where I rebuild Poker Game with more robust architecture.

The Poker Process

Development Notes

Visual Approach

As the sole developer of Poker Game (not counting codex), I had pretty much total control over how I wanted it to look and feel like. Personally, I really like when games don't try to hide the rough, sketchy parts of their art. This led me to approach the game almost like I'd approach making a painting.


Whenever a new screen or UI element needed to be made, I'd lay it out in a Procreate canvas with a screen size reference, and paint the scene, separating sprites into layers so I can export them later.

While I obviously can't use a fully painterly approach, this recontextualized the project from something wholly outside my depth to something I felt a lot more familiar with. This way, I was easily able to apply my knowledge of color, composition and emphasis to create an appealing UI and background.


On the left, you can see the initial layout sketches for the game's flow.

Game UI

On the right is the full Game Screen UI as it appears in my drawing application. Each sprite was first sketched out, then isolated and rendered so it appears smooth on-screen. You can also see the overflow sections around the UI, where I extended each sprite so that any movements wouldn't cause the edges to leak out on to the screen.
The way I initially ported sprites to my game was incredibly goofy. With zero game development experience and only the most rudimentary knowledge of Python and Pygame, I used Codex as a crutch for development and made some... questionable choices.
For instance, when exporting sprites and putting them in folders, I simply left them uncropped so that the sprite would already be positioned correctly relative to the screen. In theory, this would allow me to have the game work visually without needing to manually tweak any sprite positions, but it lead to heavy performance issues that I simply didn't notice when I ran the game on my computer. After porting the game to the website, this little shortcut caused so much lag I was forced to completely disable background animations. It was only after I realized my mistake and cropped every sprite tightly that the game ran at an acceptable framerate.

Profiles and Story Concepts

I started the game being super ambitious. My idea was for the game to be chock full of personality, to the point where despite just being Poker, there would be enough easter eggs and little details that the game could function as something interesting and standalone.
Alas, I wasn't able to incorporate absolutely every idea I had. However, the character profiles were a pretty large part of what I originally intended, and I did successfully add these to the game. Each character has a funny little backstory associated with them. Abby is trying to pay for her grandma's medicine. Susan owns the casino, Rebecca is an overqualified high school teacher, Interbob comes from the rift, so on and so forth. I don't know what Melon's deal is. I suppose he's like the player character, coming here just to have fun and all that.
In any case, I'm actually quite happy with the character profile pictures and imbued a good amount of personality into each one.

Cards, Future, and Extra

Perhaps the goofiest part of it all was the process I took to develop the cards. I actually went ahead and drew all 52 cards in the game manually. I think that alone probably took me around 6-7 hours of work. Each rank has a distinct theming that the suits abide by. 7's are fleshy, 5's are doodle-y, 9's were written on someone's homework, and the face cards are also custom made. Despite seeming like a waste of time, I actually really like that I put so much work into the cards.
Some features I have planned for the future are audio for the game, visible chips that move around the table when betting/winning happens, cutscenes, custom name entry fields (I hate the default black box that's currently there instead), and some sort of win/lose screen, since right now the game just softlocks when one person wins.
Besides that, I got nothin' else to show you. Toodle-oo.
